A Red Sox Fan Explains His Love For The Cubs

Tonight is Game Seven of the World Series — the Chicago Cubs are one win away from baseball salvation. I’m incredibly excited and anxious, more so than if my hometown team the Boston Red Sox were in the series. My hopes and dreams for the National League team from Chicago are so great that I’d be rooting for them even were they playing Boston. Really. So why the Cubs? It’s a question I’ve had to answer a few times. The long answer is that I’ve became a serious Cubs fan since July 31, 2004 — that’s the day that my home team and first love traded away my favorite ballplayer, Nomar Garciaparra. It is not coincidentally the last time I had a favorite player. I was sick about it. It was the end of watching baseball in the way I did as a kid. I was a grown man, old enough to not get attached to players, but the kid in me who sorted baseball cards and pored over their still had a soft spot for this young throwback ballplayer. By 2004, the cynicism of writers like the Boston Globe’s Dan Shaughnessy and the entitled whining of the city about the Sox had become overwhelming and it was becoming somewhat joyless to watch the team. Speaking with fellow fans, there was a complete lack of perspective or acknowledgment that we had long ceased to be lovable underdogs but were in fact the second richest team in the league. The bloodless attitude that had arrived in town with the new ownership and inspired by the brutally cutthroat (but effective) methods of the Patriots was frankly probably necessary — but not much fun to root for.
